Incense pellets — also known as *xiang wan* (香丸), kneaded incense, or nerikoh — are small hand-rolled balls of ground botanicals and resins made for gentle heating rather than burning. Unlike sticks, which smolder and release their fragrance quickly, pellets are designed to unfold slowly over hours on an electric warmer.

If you are new to heating incense pellets, the single most important thing to get right is **temperature**. Too low, and the aroma barely rises; too high, and you burn off the delicate top notes before they can bloom.

## The Ideal Temperature Range

The working range for incense pellets is roughly **100–200°C**, and the classic approach is to start low and rise gradually:

| Stage | Temperature | What you smell |
|---|---|---|
| Start | 100–120°C | Cooling top notes emerge — fresh, clear, often resinous |
| Middle | 120–160°C | Heart notes unfold — warm, sweet, balsamic |
| Final | 160–200°C | Base notes release — deep, woody, lingering |

Because every electric warmer differs in how its sensor reads the true surface temperature, treat these numbers as **reference points, not absolutes**. If your warmer runs hot, start ten degrees lower than you think you need.

## Why Start Low and Rise Slowly

A well-made incense pellet is blended in layers — light, volatile top notes; a sweet, resinous heart; and a deep woody base. Each layer vaporizes at a different temperature. Starting at 100–120°C lets the top notes breathe first; as they fade, raising the temperature progressively calls up the heart and then the base. One pellet can fragrance a room for ten hours or more when heated this way.

This is also why the pellets were resized smaller after the first batch — a smaller surface area spreads the aroma more gently, and the temperature stages become easier to read.

## Adjusting to Your Taste

There is no single "correct" strength. A thousand noses, a thousand preferences:

- **Want it stronger?** Use two pellets at once and raise the temperature slowly — the top, heart, and base notes each unfold at their own stage.
- **Want it lighter?** Break a pellet and flatten it before heating. More surface area at the same temperature means a softer, more diffuse scent.
- **Notice it fading?** That is the cue to nudge the temperature up, not to add more pellets.

## Common Questions

### How long do incense pellets last?
A single pellet heated gently can release fragrance for **ten-plus hours**, changing character as the temperature rises. Two pellets at once give a stronger, faster presence.

### Do I need a special burner?
Incense pellets need an **electric incense warmer with temperature control** — the type used for resins and loose incense. A stick holder will not work.

### What if my warmer has no temperature display?
Use the lowest setting to start, and only nudge it up when the aroma fades. Smell is the best thermometer: the moment a stage feels "done," the next one is ready.

### Can I use pellets with charcoal?
Traditional ash-bed and charcoal methods work beautifully for pellets, but they require more skill to control temperature. The electric warmer is the beginner-friendly path and the most consistent.
